No. 01 Brand Creation

Strategy before decoration.

[Author name] · [Publish date] · [Reading time]

01 The Grandyra Journal

Issue 01 of the Grandyra Journal.

Every designer knows the temptation. A brief arrives, and before the meeting is over there are already sketches in the margin of the notebook. A colour. A shape. A typeface that would look good on the sign.

We try hard to resist it. Not because sketches are bad, but because a sketch made too early answers a question nobody has asked yet.

Decoration is easy to agree on

It's much easier to have an opinion about a logo than about a positioning statement. Logos are visible; strategy is a paragraph in a document. So meetings drift towards what people can see, and the hard decisions get made quietly by accident.

That's how a company ends up with a beautiful identity that says the wrong thing — premium when the business is built on value, playful when the customers want reassurance.

The work becomes beautiful once it becomes clear.

What we ask first

  • What has to change for this business in the next three years?
  • Who are we trying to convince, and what do they believe today?
  • What can this brand say that its competitors can't?
  • What would we be embarrassed to promise?

The answers are rarely glamorous. But they turn creative work from a matter of taste into a matter of judgement — and judgement is something a client and an agency can actually discuss.

Then the beautiful part

Strategy before decoration doesn't mean strategy instead of decoration. Craft matters enormously. People feel the difference between a typeface chosen with care and one chosen in a hurry, even if they can't name it.

It just means the craft has a job to do. When it does, it lasts.
